Basins, barriers & memory

Waddington pictured development as a ball rolling through a branching landscape. Here the metaphor becomes a concrete stochastic dynamical system: a population of points moves down a potential, with optional noise.

dX = −∇V(X) dt + √(2D) dW

Start with the terrain

In Landscape studio, hollow rings are editable well sources. Filled dots are the actual minima of the combined potential. They can move away from the sources, and some sources may produce no separate minimum. Raise smoothing, drag sources together or change a depth to see basins merge or vanish.

The studio combines quadratic wells through a smooth minimum:

V(x, y) = −s log Σᵢ exp[(hᵢ − ½‖(x, y) − cᵢ‖²)/s] − bx

Here s controls rounding, hᵢ deepens a source, and b tilts the terrain. Noise D is a separate parameter. The basin graph follows the two downhill branches of each saddle to their neighboring minima. It can contain cycles; a branching tree is not imposed.

Then add noise

At D = 0, cells follow gradient descent. With noise, they can leave a basin without any minimum disappearing. A saddle’s height above a minimum is the directional barrier ΔV. The ratio ΔV/D helps compare escape difficulty; exp(−ΔV/D) is a barrier factor, not a complete transition rate.

At a fixed potential and D > 0, the stationary density is proportional to exp(−V/D). White ticks integrate that density over the sampled basins within the displayed window. They are equilibrium references, not predictions of an immediate outcome. The evolving population may remain far from equilibrium.

A fold makes a fate disappear

Fold & memory uses V = x⁴/4 − ax²/2 − bx + y²/2. Critical x coordinates satisfy x³ − ax − b = 0. For a > 0 there are two minima and a saddle when |b| < 2(a/3)³ᐟ². At either endpoint, a minimum and saddle meet with a zero Hessian eigenvalue.

Try Quiet tipping and start a round trip. In the Hysteresis view, the population’s forward and return paths overlay the stationary branches. Solid branches are stable minima; the dashed branch is a saddle. Finite sweep rate can delay tipping beyond the static fold. Noise can induce earlier escapes and smooth out the memory loop. Compare rates and noise levels.

What is computed, and what is approximate?

The quartic critical points and fold thresholds are analytic. Studio critical points come from a multistart Newton search with analytic gradients and Hessians. Classification uses the Hessian eigenvalues. A finite search can miss critical points, especially near degeneracy. The minima − saddles + maxima check should equal 1 away from degeneracies for these confining plane potentials; passing this check alone does not prove completeness.

Basin colors use steepest descent on a 172 × 116 grid, so boundaries are approximate. Saddle connections use continuous gradient descent. Cells follow Euler–Maruyama with step 0.01 and extra substeps in the stiff quartic tails. Trajectories have no reflecting boundary. Cells outside the view are counted separately. These are dimensionless illustrative models; inference about real developmental rates needs a calibrated model.
