Goal

Each case has its own suspects, relics, rooms, story, floor plan, and Mythos events. The investigator cast and core rules persist from case to case.

Find the one hidden Suspect, Relic, Room, and Entity before a rival investigator does, or before the Doom clock expires. The available limits are 10 and 15. Reaching the limit does not end the case immediately: it begins one final full investigator round. Every non-solution Evidence card is held by one of the four investigators.

Keep these actions separate: Move, Action-card plays, your once-per-turn Suggestion, once-per-room Investigation, and Resolve actions are different things. Using one normally does not spend the others.

Your Turn

Start of turn: draw Action cards until you have at least five in hand. Move and Resolve reset to 0. Most investigators lose any unspent Insight between turns. Inspector Eliza Graves may carry up to 1 unspent Insight into her next turn; Nathaniel Orne begins each turn with 1 Insight. You normally have two Action-card plays.

1. Roll Movement: roll one die. The result becomes your Move for the turn.

2. Act in any useful order: after rolling, you may interleave movement, card plays, Investigation, your Suggestion, Resolve actions, and Market purchase as their requirements allow.

3. Move: click an adjacent legal square to spend 1 Move. Movement is orthogonal through corridors and doorways. Portal and creature squares are occupied spaces: investigator meeples may not enter or pass through those squares and must route around them. Door thresholds are kept clear of portals and creatures. Undo Move rewinds ordinary movement one step at a time, restoring both your previous square and the Move you had at that point; press it repeatedly to walk your route backward as far as the stored movement history allows. The history is cleared when movement reveals a new room scene, triggers a creature encounter, or you take another consequential action. Portals, Impossible Shortcuts, and Common Interior crossings are not part of the undo history. Entering a room you have never visited before ends ordinary movement for the turn. Returning to a room your investigator has already occupied does not end movement, so you may continue spending any Move you have left. Your starting room counts as already visited. If you begin your turn in a room, you may use that room first and then leave with your remaining Move. A Suggestion or Investigation does not itself erase unused Move.

4. Investigate rooms: each investigator may investigate each room once per game. Investigation grants 1 Insight, reveals that room's investigation scene, and does not prevent movement or a Suggestion in the same turn. The Pallid Watcher blocks Investigation until banished.

5. Make at most one Suggestion: a Suggestion is independent of Investigation. You may investigate and suggest in the same room and same turn, in either order. Every creature blocks Suggestions until banished.

6. Resolve actions: sealing a portal or banishing a creature does not consume your Suggestion or Investigation. These may be taken whenever their location and Resolve requirements are met.

7. Buy at most one Market card: spend Insight equal to its cost. The purchased card enters your hand immediately. If you still have a legal card play remaining, you may use it that turn. If you had already reached your current card-play limit when you bought it, the new card is locked until your next turn—even if it is Quick. You may still retain it normally.

8. End Turn: the retention screen is only a preview until you press Confirm End Turn. Use Cancel — Keep Playing if you forgot to move, play a card, buy, banish, or seal. On confirmation, retain up to two unplayed cards and discard the rest. Unplayed Madness is retained automatically and uses those slots. If a creature still occupies your room, you gain its listed lingering Exposure (normally 1; Chromatic Miasma causes 2).

Action Cards

Each investigator begins with a 10-card starter deck: four Measured Steps, three Patient Inquiries, two Composure, and one Desperate Pursuit. The investigator-panel label Action Cards means the total cards you own across hand, deck, and discard pile; it is not the number of actions remaining this turn.

Most Action cards use one of your two normal card plays. Quick cards do not use a normal play, but only one Quick card may be played per turn. Careful Planning raises your normal play limit by one for that turn.

Desperate Pursuit: every investigator begins with one. It always gives +1 Move. If you have an unplayed Madness card in hand, you may discard one to gain +3 additional Move, for +4 Move total. Discarding the Madness this way avoids that Madness card's printed cost.

Insight and the Market

Insight is temporary deckbuilding currency. It is meant to be earned during the turn rather than received automatically. Each room can provide 1 Insight once per investigator through Investigation. When a rival refutes your Suggestion by showing you an Evidence card you did not already know, you gain 1 Insight; seeing the same card again gives no standard Insight. Patient Inquiry and other Action cards, sealing portals, and banishing creatures can also generate it. Market cards cost 2–4 Insight, and you may buy only one per turn. Most investigators lose all unspent Insight between turns. Eliza retains up to 1 unspent Insight; Nathaniel instead begins every turn with 1 Insight. The shared Market contains two copies of each of 20 upgrade types; five are face-up at a time until the supply is exhausted.

Resolve, Nerve, and Creatures

Nerve is a permanent character bonus used only for creature tests. It is never spent. Whenever you enter a room containing a creature, test:

d6 + Resolve + Nerve − Exposure penalty ≥ 5

Resolve contributes to the test but is not spent by it. At Exposure 3–4, the Exposure penalty is 1. A failed creature test adds Panic and 1 Exposure.

Banish Creature: most creatures cost 2 Resolve to banish; the Knotted Horror costs 3. You may banish at any point during your turn whenever you share the same physical room-space and have enough Resolve. Banishment removes the creature and grants 2 Insight. It does not consume movement, Investigation, Suggestion, or Final Accusation. Every creature blocks Suggestions and portal travel until banished, and a creature must also be cleared before a Final Accusation can be made from that room-space.

Creature position and movement: a creature occupies one literal board square, which investigators cannot enter. Creatures never occupy a door square or the interior square immediately behind a door. A creature produced by portal pressure appears beside that portal; during the Mythos phase of the following round it moves once, up to 3 legal orthogonal squares within that room, tending away from the portal. It will not move onto an investigator, portal, creature, or doorway-clearance square.

Creature traits: Pallid Watcher blocks both Suggestion and Investigation; Knotted Horror costs 3 Resolve to banish; Chromatic Miasma causes 2 Exposure if you end a turn with it; Whisper Leech removes 1 Insight when you fail its creature test. Monster type is public information—hover its meeple or read the encounter scene.

Portals

A portal joins two distant rooms, and each endpoint occupies a literal numbered square inside its room. Portal squares are walkable. To choose and use a portal, move your investigator onto that specific endpoint; the Use Portal button then names its destination. The ordinary step onto the endpoint is the portal's movement cost, so clicking Use Portal does not cost a second Move. Portal travel then ends ordinary movement for the turn, gives 1 Exposure, and normally adds a Madness card. Mae Wren does not gain that portal Madness. Secret Architecture makes the step onto a portal endpoint cost 0 Move that turn. If two or more portals occupy the same room, walking onto one is how you choose which connection to use. A creature in the room still prevents portal travel until it is banished.

Open portals are dangerous during every Mythos phase. After the ordinary Mythos event and any Case II Geometry Pulse, each open portal rolls a d6 independently. On 1–4, it holds. On 5–6, a creature comes through at one available endpoint. If one endpoint space already contains a creature, the new creature appears at the other. If both endpoint spaces are occupied, the breach convulses but cannot admit another creature. A portal that opens during the current Mythos phase is immediately subject to this check. Open portals no longer add Doom separately here; instead, every open portal makes the single Doom check more dangerous.

Seal Portal: spend 1 Resolve while in its physical room-space. If there is only one portal there, you may seal it from anywhere in the room as before. If there are multiple portals in that room-space, stand on the endpoint you intend to seal so the choice is unambiguous. Sealing grants 1 Insight, removes that portal from the board, and permanently removes its future Mythos-pressure roll. Abigail Ward gains 1 additional Insight whenever she seals a portal. A portal may still be sealed while a creature is present. Every open portal in the room-space must be sealed before a Final Accusation can be made there.

An Impossible Shortcut is a temporary favorable distortion: it connects two rooms until the next Mythos phase. It can form only between rooms that are completely empty when it appears—no investigator meeple, portal endpoint, or creature may be in either room. The two eligible rooms are chosen randomly, favoring distant pairs when possible. If fewer than two empty rooms exist, no Shortcut forms. Crossing it costs 1 Move, ends ordinary movement, and causes no Exposure or Madness. Gold diamond markers show its two ends.

Evidence and the Casebook

Each current case contains 24 possibilities: five Suspects, five Relics, nine Rooms, and five Entities. One card from each category is sealed as the solution, leaving 20 Evidence cards. Those 20 are dealt evenly, so each investigator begins knowing five eliminations.

When another investigator privately shows you a refutation, it becomes known Evidence immediately whether or not you file it yet. If that card is new to you, gain 1 Insight. A repeated card gives no standard Insight. Known Evidence can never be marked suspicious. Filing it crosses that possibility out in the Casebook.

Suspicion markers: you may mark at most one suspicion per category. Clicking a different possibility in the same category moves the marker; clicking the currently marked possibility clears it. Suspicion markers are notes, not proof. They pre-fill Suggestion and Final Accusation choices when possible.

Your investigator-panel Evidence count is the number of distinct Evidence cards you know. It starts at 5 and increases when you see a new refutation; seeing the same card twice does not increase it.

Suggestions

You may make one Suggestion per turn. You must be physically inside the room you are suggesting about. Choose a Suspect, Relic, and Entity; normally the Room is automatically the room you occupy. Investigation is separate, so you may investigate the room and make a Suggestion there in the same turn. In The Geometry Below, two temporarily merged rooms count as one shared interior, so while standing in either half you may test either room identity in your Suggestion.

Starting with the next investigator clockwise, rivals check their Evidence. The first investigator who can refute must privately reveal exactly one matching card. If they hold more than one match, they choose which one to reveal.

If nobody else can refute, the game compares the suggestion with Evidence you already know. If even one named card is known Evidence, the theory is disproved. If none is known Evidence, the theory is logically complete: every non-solution card is held by an investigator, so those four unrefuted unknown cards must be the solution.

Rival deductions are public information. The Chronicle records every AI investigator's full Suggestion and the identity of the investigator who refuted it, if any. The actual card shown remains private. Rival investigators also follow different habits: Graves systematically tests suspects, Vale and Wren emphasize entities, Ward and Peabody emphasize relics, Wren seeks portals, Peabody seeks creatures, Ward responds to portals and creatures, and Orne favors the shortest useful route.

Final Accusation

A Final Accusation must name the Room you physically occupy. You cannot accuse a distant room: travel there first. In Case II, an active Common Interior counts as one shared physical space, so either joined Room identity may be named while you occupy either half. The accusation must also be made from a stable room-space: not a corridor, and with no open portal or creature remaining there. Seal and/or banish first if necessary.

You may accuse during your own turn, including before rolling, but not in the same turn in which you made a Suggestion. A clean unrefuted Suggestion is preserved and pre-fills the accusation on your next turn. Sealing and banishing are free-form Resolve actions and do not consume the accusation.

A correct Final Accusation wins immediately. A wrong human accusation ends the game. An AI investigator who accuses incorrectly can no longer win. When Doom first reaches the limit selected before the case, all four investigators receive one final full round. When that round ends, the clock expires and everyone loses unless somebody has solved the case.

Exposure and Madness

Exposure 0–2: no direct penalty. Exposure 3–4: −1 on creature tests. Exposure 5: suffer an Occult Breakdown: add Panic, reset Exposure to 2, and skip your next full turn.

Madness cards dilute your Action deck. Once drawn, unplayed Madness is sticky: it is automatically retained at end of turn and occupies retention space. You now have three ways to live with one: leave it in hand and let it clog retention, play it for a dangerous benefit, or discard it through Desperate Pursuit. Panic gives +3 Move and +1 Exposure. Fractured Memory gives +2 Resolve, then costs 1 Insight; if you have none to lose, it gives +1 Exposure instead. Whispers Between Pages gives +2 Insight and +1 Exposure. Generic Madness effects and portal crossings may add any of the three; failed creature tests still specifically add Panic.

Mythos and the Changing University

After all four investigators act, one card is drawn from the case's shuffled Mythos deck and its event resolves. Mythos cards are not repeated until that deck is exhausted and reshuffled. The card itself does not automatically add Doom. After the event, portal pressure, and any Case II Geometry Pulse resolve, make one Doom check: roll a d10 and add +1 for each open portal. A natural 1 always means no Doom. A modified result of 7–9 adds 1 Doom; 10 or more adds 2 Doom; anything lower adds none. Thus sealing portals directly buys the investigators time. Each case is arranged so that a creature event appears within the first two Mythos phases. In Case I, at least one portal-producing event also appears within the first three Mythos phases.

Geometry Pulse — Case II: after the ordinary Mythos card resolves, the annex independently tests whether its geometry changes again. At Doom 0–2, a d6 result of 4–6 causes a distortion (50%). At Doom 3–5, 2–6 causes one (5/6). At Doom 6+, a distortion happens every Mythos phase; on a second roll of 5–6, a second different distortion occurs as well. The possible pulse effects are a relocated door, a room exchange, a Common Interior, or an Impossible Shortcut. The affected rooms and exact effect remain random.

Architectural changes are accepted only when every room remains connected to the corridor network, so the university may become impossible without permanently trapping an investigator. Doorways also maintain a clear threshold: if a moving door would place a portal or creature on the door square or immediately in front of it, that portal/creature is automatically displaced to the nearest legal square in the same room. Room exchanges similarly re-seat their spatial markers inside the room's new geometry. If a doorway moves in the room you occupy, gain 1 Exposure. If the room you occupy physically exchanges position with another room, gain 1 Exposure. A ward may absorb this Exposure normally.

Merged rooms — Case II: a Common Interior lasts for the next two full rounds. The paired rooms remain two Evidence identities but function as one physical space. You may cross directly between them for 1 Move without ending ordinary movement, use a portal located in either half, banish a creature in either half, and choose either room identity for a Suggestion. The danger is shared too: a creature in either half blocks the merged space and can cause lingering Exposure from the other half. The two-round duration is used so every investigator gets the same opportunity to exploit or suffer the distortion.
