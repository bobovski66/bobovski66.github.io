A little orientation

Follow the flow.

Choose an example or type your own equations. Click the field to start a solution. Change a parameter and watch every trajectory respond. The colored curves show solutions; the moving dots follow increasing time.

Two ways to explore

Direction field: enter y′ = f(t,y). The horizontal axis is time. A click sets both t₀ and y₀.
Phase plane: enter an autonomous system x′ = f(x,y), y′ = g(x,y). Each click sets (x₀,y₀) at time zero. The linked plot shows its coordinates over time. In direction-field mode, Δt on the animation control is time elapsed from each initial condition; the plot axes use actual time.

Equation syntax

+ − * / ^Arithmetic and powers. -x^2 means -(x^2).
2x, x*y2x works; write x*y for variable products.
sin, cos, tanTrig functions use radians. Also asin, acos, atan, atan2.
exp, log, ln, log10log and ln are natural logarithms.
sqrt, abs, tanhAlso sinh, cosh, floor, ceil, sign, min, max, pow.
pi, e, a, b, c, dConstants and parameters. Used parameters become sliders.

Use parentheses for function arguments: sin(t), max(0,y). Equations are parsed as mathematics; JavaScript is not accepted.

Reading a phase portrait

Nullclines mark where one derivative vanishes. Their intersections can be equilibria. Select an equilibrium for its numerical Jacobian, eigenvalues, and linear stability classification. For a saddle, trace the four local branches along its eigenvectors. Purely imaginary or zero eigenvalues require nonlinear analysis.

Numerical details

Trajectories use adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4), with your relative tolerance and absolute tolerance equal to one hundredth of it. The maximum time step also controls resolution. Equilibria are found by a finite numerical search, so some may be missed. Nullclines are sampled contours. Stiff equations and behavior near singularities may need a smaller step, tighter tolerance, or a specialized solver.
